# Mascot and Brand Asset Brief

## Objective
Create an original, friendly illustrated character that gives the product warmth and recognisability without making a financial utility feel childish or unserious.

## Character direction
- Friendly, calm, capable and quietly playful.
- Simple distinctive silhouette that reads at small sizes.
- Original character, not a robot, generic AI assistant, animal copied from a known brand or an existing app mascot.
- Restrained palette aligned with the dark interface; use the accent green sparingly.
- Strong outline and shape language that works on dark and light backgrounds.
- Avoid overly complex detail, glossy 3D, random gradients and generic stock illustration style.

## Required poses/states
1. Welcome/onboarding: inviting, neutral pose.
2. Empty subscriptions: gently encouraging.
3. Adding a subscription: holding/pointing to a simple calendar or checklist prop.
4. Reminder upcoming: attentive, not alarmist.
5. Successful save: small celebratory gesture.
6. No search result: helpful and calm.
7. Error/offline: reassuring, not comical.
8. Small app icon/brand mark adaptation.

## Asset production process
1. Create a one-page character concept sheet with front, side and three-quarter views.
2. Approve silhouette, face, palette and proportions before producing many poses.
3. Create a consistent vector master (SVG or equivalent editable source).
4. Export transparent PNG/WebP sizes for app use.
5. If animated, prefer Rive for interactive state-based animation; Lottie is acceptable for simple non-interactive sequences.
6. Keep animation subtle and performant. Provide static fallback and reduced-motion behaviour.
7. Store source files and licences/creation provenance in `branding/`.

## Do not
- Generate final character assets before the user approves a concept.
- Use the mascot as decoration on every screen.
- Let the mascot obscure information or actions.
- Use AI-generated images as fake provider logos.
- Assume brand name or mascot name; both remain undecided.
